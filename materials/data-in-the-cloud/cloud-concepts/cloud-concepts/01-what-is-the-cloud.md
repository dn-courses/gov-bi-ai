# What the Cloud Actually Is

🚧 **Under construction** — scaffold for the syllabus; final content to be written.

"The cloud" is not a place and not magic: it is **computing resources rented as an on-demand
service** over a network, instead of hardware you buy, rack, and operate yourself. This lesson
strips the marketing away and gives a precise working definition you can reason about.

## What this lesson covers

- The one-sentence definition: someone else's computers, plus the software to provision and bill
  them, offered as a self-service utility.
- The defining characteristics (after the NIST model): on-demand self-service, broad network
  access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, measured (metered) service.
- The shift in mental model: from **capital** purchase (buy a server, own it for years) to
  **operational** rental (turn capacity on and off, pay for what you use).
- Where it came from and why now: virtualization, cheap networking, and economies of scale.
- A first map of the big providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) and how to read their service menus
  without drowning in product names.

## Key takeaways

- The cloud is a *delivery and billing model*, not a technology you can point at.
- The five characteristics are the test: if it isn't self-service, elastic, and metered, it isn't
  really cloud.
- Everything later in the module — service models, deployment models, databases — hangs off this
  rent-vs-own shift.
